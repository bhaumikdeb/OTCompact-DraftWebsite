Before You Apply for an OT Compact Privilege

By Dennis Crayon · updated August 18, 2026

Read this page carefully.

OT Compact Eligibility Requirements:
Home State = a state that is a member of the OT Compact AND where you live (primarily reside) AND hold a license to practice as an OT/OTA in that state
• hold an unencumbered license in your Home State (see definition for “Home State” above)
• complete a Criminal Background Check (CBC) through your Home State licensing board. Contact your Home State licensing board for instructions on how to complete the CBC. The OT Compact staff does not conduct or facilitate CBCs.

Within your OTC application, you will provide legally binding attestations that you:

  • read and understand each state’s OT Laws, Rules, and Practice Act/Scope of Practice in which you apply to practice.
  • have already completed any jurisprudence requirements, as applicable.
  • designate your home state license in the state where you live (home state license = primary state of residence)

This webpage provides you access to information related to each state’s laws and jurisprudence requirements. If you have specific questions about a state’s requirements or when they plan to accept OTC applications, please reach out directly to that state’s OT licensing board (hyperlinked to state name).

The OTC application process requires you to choose a “home state license” (HSL), which is defined in law as your “primary state of residence” and a member of the OTC (OTC Legislation Sec. 2. Definitions. J). Each time you renew your HSL, you will need to purchase a new OTC privilege to practice in all of the states in which you plan to practice. Your OTC privilege(s) to practice expire when your HSL expires. If you practice in a state with an expired or deactivated OTC privilege to practice, you are subject to that state licensing board’s authority and accountability.

States not listed below are not a member of the Occupational Therapy Compact. See the OTC Map webpage for each state’s membership status.
